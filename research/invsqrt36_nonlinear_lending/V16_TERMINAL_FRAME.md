## Arithmetic design hypothesis only (not new native or novelty claim)
PR60/63 already identified redundant low residual bits and odd digit recovery. To maintain the quotient at *every* update without restoring the full residual, let a odd, B=2^w, M=B^2, P=a*y, R=C-a*y^2, C divisible by M, r=floor(R/M), s=R mod M=(-a^{-1} P^2) mod M. For appended digit d in [0,B), define K=ceil((2BdP+ad^2)/M). Then P'=BP+ad, r'=Mr+s-K. Reverse: d=(a^{-1}P') mod B, P=(P'-ad)/B, r=floor((r'+K)/M). The reverse quotient identity needs no computation of s, since 0<=s<M. For B=2, K=d(P+ceil(a/4)), s=0 if P even, otherwise (-a) mod 4. Select largest legal d with Mr+s>=K; investigate joint comparison/subtraction rather than separate compare + controlled subtract, as in COMP-N-SUB. This is an exact integer recurrence with signed floor division, not a bounded-word or KMB proof. It may save stored low residual bits but can increase gate count; a must be normalized odd; rounding, phase, actual fixed-point widths, carry cost, allocation, cleanup and native semantics all untested. Distinct from a mere one-boundary decoder by keeping the compressed quotient persistently, but source-level novelty not yet audited. No optimization search while the historical ANGLE gate is blocked.
