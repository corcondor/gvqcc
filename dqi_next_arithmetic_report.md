# DQI arithmetic: verified local improvements and remaining width obstacles

## Decision

Two new local constructions passed native KickMix checks. Neither lowers the
complete decoder's current 889-qubit peak on its own. **Do not replace the DQI
member of the proposed submission with either experiment.** Keep the public
baseline `821bf8d157c03c3e868a0099b0ab1438cfba664ab1bc6fec3210654099b672a3`.

| Experiment | Local quantum width | Static T | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Classical GF64 scaling inside fixed-root inverse phase | 12 → 10, including 6 input bits | 4 → 4 | Exact construction and native checks pass; other peaks remain |
| Special matched K24 inverse pair, with 865 retained bits including its owned bank | 889 → 877 | 386 → 409 | Native checks pass; T/width tradeoff, not a complete decoder improvement |
| Mixed-input quadratic norm COMM search | 36 → 36 | 42 → 42 | No reduction found; exact identity and native checks pass |

No complete candidate KMB or submission ZIP was generated by these experiments.
The small inverse-phase and norm KMBs are component fixtures only. The unpublished
chart-norm repair candidate is separate and was not used as the baseline here.

## 1. Classical multiplication needs no quantum workspace

The original GF64 scalar inverse-phase routine multiplies a quantum word by a
known nonzero classical scalar using a six-qubit temporary, a swap, and an
uncompute. The new `classical_scale_inplace` performs the same map using only
classically controlled CX and SWAP gates. Its working matrix and pivot decisions
are classical bits, which are outside the quantum-width cost.

Let `R(a)` be the binary matrix of multiplication by the classical field element
`a`. Gaussian elimination on `R(a^-1)` produces elementary row operations `E`
with `E R(a^-1) = I`. Applying those same row operations to the quantum word gives
`E x = R(a) x = a*x`. The caller already supplies `a` and its inverse and already
guards the zero-mask branch. The transformation preserves both classical inputs.
It is an exact linear permutation with no phase, including for entangled quantum
inputs; the nonzero scalar is the stated domain restriction.

The fixed-root scalar inverse phase retains four AND intermediates. Removing
the temporary therefore lowers its additional helpers from six to four. Its
static T remains 4 and its exact uniform-mask expected T remains `4*63/64 =
3.9375`. A quantum-controlled root still needs six AND intermediates, so that
case remains Q13/T6; it is not counted as a width improvement.

Native checks in `dqi_next_arithmetic_classical_scale_run/dqi_next_arithmetic_report.json`:

- Every 64 quantum inputs × 63 nonzero classical multipliers: 4,032 cases.
  All source products, multiplier/inverse preservation, nine unrelated dirty
  qubits, and phases agree.
- Fixed root: every 64 source × 64 mask pair, 4,096 cases for each of four HMR
  seeds. All input, mask, scratch and phase checks pass; Q12→10, T4 unchanged.
- Quantum root: all 8,192 source/mask/root triples for each of four HMR seeds.
  All checks pass; Q13/T6 unchanged, as expected.
- Fresh builds are byte-identical. The fixed-root fixture SHA256 is
  `74855b6971fa8435847055ad285cf4a819ea9e7cf86f36f4d3214b516ee8dd28`.

## 2. The special matched inverse can reuse its owned bank

The selected first-three-chart source provider already owns a clean 24-bit carry
bank. Its original matched inverse pair uses up to 24 *additional* helpers.
The new schedule keeps that ownership contract and reduces the additional peak
to 12 by clearing the child norms before consuming the input halves.

For an input `S` in K24, define:

\[
N=\operatorname{Norm}_{24/12}(S),\quad R=\operatorname{inv0}(N),\quad
C=\operatorname{Norm}_{12/6}(N),\quad D=\operatorname{inv0}(C).
\]

The field identity `D = Norm(R)` holds including zero. Once both `N` and `R`
exist, `C` can be HMR-erased and its phase repaid from `N`; `D` can likewise be
erased from `R`. Both are quadratic norm phase repayments with zero additional
T. The owned bank's last 12 bits are now clean and can serve as the temporary
for the two source-half products. The original norm `N` stays live to repay the
half-product masks.

| Stage | Contents of caller-owned bank24 | Extra helpers |
|---|---|---:|
| Build child inverse | N12 + C6 + D6 | At most 10 for cubic inverse |
| Build reciprocal norm | N12 + C6 + D6 | R12 |
| Erase children | N12 + clean12 | R12 |
| Consume source halves | N12 + temporary12 | R12 |
| Erase N and repay return phase | clean24, lent to Phase12 | R12 |
| Finish | clean24 | 0 |

The prefix HMR-erases `N` in **polynomial basis** and saves its 12-bit mask.
The matching return's reciprocal norm is exactly the prefix's original `N`,
so the saved phase is repaid by a classically controlled CZ. The return's own
norm mask is repaid using the existing 27-T inverse-phase routine on `R`.
An explicit `CleanBankLender` gives that routine the already-owned clean bank;
merely leaving the bank zero while requesting new allocations would not reduce
the peak. The lender never frees the caller's ownership and verifies that all
borrowed wires have been returned.

An independent read-only algebra review checked the norm/inverse identities,
zero extension and mask basis. It also exhaustively checked the relevant
GF4096 norm identities on all 4,096 elements.

### Measured local tradeoff

| Metric | Existing selected special pair | New bank schedule |
|---|---:|---:|
| Prefix static T | 191 | 191 |
| Return static T | 195 | 218 |
| Pair static T | 386 | 409 |
| Exact uniform-HMR mean T | 385.9375 | 408.993408203125 |
| Additional helpers beyond owned retained data | 24 | 12 |
| Native peak with 865 retained qubits | 889 | 877 |
| Saved prefix classical mask | 6 bits | 12 bits |

The mean increase per pair is **23.055908203125 T**. There is no claim that the
complete decoder becomes Q877. Integrating this component alone would retain
other Q889 peaks while adding T, so it is not selected. The documented source
has three eligible special pairs; replacing only those would add 69 static T,
or 69.167724609375 expected T, without a demonstrated global-width benefit.

### Native validation

`dqi_next_arithmetic_bank_inverse.json` records 16,384 inputs per seed: all
4,096 low-only K24 words, all 4,096 high-only words, and 8,192 fixed random general
K24 words. This includes zero but is not an exhaustive test of all K24 values.

Both old and new pairs were run with four HMR seeds: **131,072 pair shots total**.
Between prefix and return, the circuit applies a classical-mask phase probe and
a two-qubit CZ, with the expected phase independently calculated from `inv0(S)`.
This prevents an incorrect prefix followed by its own inverse from passing only
because their errors cancel. Source values, phase, all 24 bank bits, 817 unrelated
dirty bits, classical probes and every extra helper have zero errors.

Both prefixes were also checked on all 16,384 inputs for the exact inverse value,
bank cleanup and other restoration. Their intentionally deferred phases were
not incorrectly treated as closed phases. No full-decoder official validation
or failure probability is inferred from these component checks.

## 3. Why the complete 889-qubit peak remains

The public circuit and supplied source family supply independent evidence.
The width audit freshly hashes the **actual public DQI821** file and revalidates
two full opcode-and-operand templates in that binary. It does not infer the
public circuit's contents from an unpublished candidate's name.

The saved complete exact-template audit of that identical public SHA256 contains:

| Kernel | All exact matches | Output includes q888 | Freshly revalidated witness, operation interval |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| GF12 accumulator, 42 CCX | 3,823 | 475 | `[11156836, 11158154)` |
| GF24 accumulator, 99 CCX | 1,376 | 188 | `[11232055, 11238252)` |

The GF12 witness has its 12-bit norm operand on q865..q876 and its 12-bit
temporary output on q877..q888. The GF24 witness has physical input q72..q95,
carry input q840..q863, and a 24-bit temporary on exactly q865..q888 (in its
kernel's permuted order). The 188/475 totals are from the prior complete audit;
this task freshly revalidated the displayed witnesses and the entire KMB hash.

Of the other GF24 matches, 1,184 write the existing carry q840..q863 and four
have maximum output index q887. Thus 1,376 is not the number of consuming-buffer
peak sites that necessarily need replacing. The 188 peak placements are a more
targeted future search set. Gate appearances are not a proof of an optimal width
or a proof that every peak must be addressed by a separate rewrite.

### Native live-allocation measurements

`dqi_next_arithmetic_width_audit.py` wraps the actual native builder and records
each allocation, its source call stack and all simultaneously owned helpers.
It uses 865 retained bits, corresponding to the source family's 840 syndrome,
24 carry and one rolling predicate. Replacing the GF64 classical scales produces:

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| GF24 consuming prefix | 889 | 889 | 99 / 99 |
| GF24 consuming prefix and closing | 889 | 889 | 459 / 459 |
| GF24 deferred inverse | 889 | 889 | 192 / 192 |
| Direct division plus next-word return, including next input prefix | 889 | 889 | 933 / 933 |
| Direct division plus retained-child final return | 889 | 889 | 643 / 643 |
| GF24 affine quotient phase | 889 | 889 | 261 / 261 |
| Outer norm plus GF12 inverse | 889 | 889 | 108 / 108 |
| Outer norm plus GF12 inverse phase | 889 | 889 | 69 / 69 |
| Outer norm + child + GF64 inverse phase | 889 | 887 | 61 / 61 |

These traces identify distinct surviving causes, not just a single maximum:

- `gf24_nested_norm.py:269` and `:305`, and
  `direct_carry_arithmetic.py:37`: a full **24-bit consuming output buffer**.
- `nested_norm_inverse.py:33`: nested norms **12 + 6 + 3**, together with a
  **3-bit temporary**, total 24.
- `nested_norm_inverse.py:82`: **outer norm12 + child6 + temporary6**, total 24.
- `gf24_nested_norm.py:103` / `:321` and
  `direct_carry_arithmetic.py:50`: **reciprocal norm12 + temporary12**, total 24.
- `phase_inverse12.py:60` / `:28`: **outer norm12 + B4 + C4 + four leaf AND
  bits**, total 24. The old classical scale also reaches 12+12, but removing
  that scale alone leaves this separate leaf-stage peak.

The GF64 replacement changes the last row's 12+6+6 to 12+6+4. It leaves every
other listed composition intact. The bank schedule resolves a different special
inverse lifetime, but does not alter the generic consuming products or nested
inverse lifetimes listed above.

The source-family component measurements are not a claim that the whole supplied
generator emits DQI821 byte for byte. The actual-public binary witnesses and
the component allocation evidence are deliberately reported with their separate
scopes. Full source hashes and peak-owner records are in the audit JSON.

## 4. Bounded norm search

The COMM experiment allowed mixed linear forms in the 24 input bits of the
quadratic norm, rather than keeping the two input-factor subspaces separated.
The executed search completed in 24.965 seconds and stayed at 42 AND terms.
There was no 42→41 reduction.

All 301 degree-at-most-two coefficients were checked, proving equality for every
one of the 2^24 inputs and every initial XOR accumulator. Native verification
also passed 16,384 arbitrary-target cases and a second restoring pass with no
value or phase errors. These results certify the unchanged function, not a
gate improvement or a lower-bound theorem.

## Next candidate, without starting another search

A complete width improvement needs a schedule for the actual consuming-product
sites and generic nested norm/half-scale sites, followed by a fresh lifetime
check of the remaining inverse-phase helpers. The verified owned-bank technique
can be reused where a clean bank is already part of the caller's contract.
It must not be extended to an arbitrary dirty carry. Simply splitting a GF24
output into 12-bit halves is insufficient: the first overwritten input half
is still needed by the second output unless a new reversible factorization or
an independently proved retained witness is supplied.

No additional search or full integration was started after these results.
